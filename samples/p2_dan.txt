Interviewer: Can you tell me about your referral?

P2: Mine was quick, which I know is not everyone's experience. I went to the GP on the Tuesday about the numbness in my hand and I had a letter by the Friday with an appointment for the following month. So about five weeks all in.

Interviewer: Why do you think it went smoothly for you?

P2: Honestly? Luck, and my GP. She rang the department herself while I was sitting there. She said, I know the secretary, let me try. I could hear her talking to somebody. That one phone call is the difference between my story and my neighbour's story, and my neighbour waited fourteen months for something similar. That does not feel like a system. That feels like knowing the right person.

Interviewer: How did you feel during the wait?

P2: Fine, relaxed really. I knew the date, I knew who I was seeing, I could plan work around it. Five weeks is nothing when you know it is five weeks. I told my wife, look, it is the twelfth, put it in the calendar, and then I stopped thinking about it.

Interviewer: What was the appointment itself like?

P2: Very good. He explained the nerve thing clearly, drew me a diagram, gave me exercises, told me exactly what to do if it got worse and who to call. I felt looked after. I came out thinking, that is what it is supposed to be like.

Interviewer: Has anything about the experience stayed with you?

P2: The guilt, a bit. That sounds odd. But when you hear what other people go through and you got seen in five weeks because your GP happened to have a mobile number, you feel like you jumped a queue you did not know you were in. I would rather everyone got the letter in three days than me getting it because of a favour.

Interviewer: Do you have confidence in the service now?

P2: In the people, yes, completely. The staff were excellent, every single one, from the woman on reception to the consultant. In the arrangements, less so. It works when somebody personally makes it work. Take that person out and I do not think the process holds itself up.

Interviewer: Anything you would change?

P2: Tell people where they are. That is all. My father-in-law is waiting for a knee and he has heard nothing for seven months and he has started to assume he has been forgotten. He probably has not been forgotten, but you cannot blame him for thinking it. Silence does that to people. Five weeks of knowing was easier than seven months of wondering would ever be.
