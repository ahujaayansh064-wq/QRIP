Interviewer: Could you tell me about your experience of being referred?

P4: There is not a great deal to tell, love. I was referred in the autumn and I was seen this spring. That is how it goes now. I am seventy-eight, I have been dealing with the health service since before you were born, and I do not expect it to be quick.

Interviewer: Did you hear anything during that time?

P4: One letter, in the December, saying I was on the list. That is more than some get. I did not chase because there is no sense in chasing. They are not sitting there withholding an appointment from me out of spite. There are not enough of them and there are too many of us. Ringing up and shouting at a girl on reception does not build a hospital.

Interviewer: How did you find the waiting?

P4: You get on with it. I did my garden. I kept my appointments with the GP for the blood pressure. My wife worried more than I did, she is a worrier, and I told her, it will come when it comes. I have had worse waits for less.

Interviewer: Did the wait affect you at all?

P4: The hip got worse, obviously. I stopped walking down to the club because I could not manage the hill both ways, and that is where I see people. So I saw fewer people. I would not have thought of that as the wait doing something to me but I suppose it was. My son says I got quieter. I do not know about that.

Interviewer: What was the appointment like when it came?

P4: Very efficient. In, examined, x-rayed, out. The young doctor was pleasant enough. He said I should have been referred a year earlier, and I said, well, I was not, so where does that leave us. He did not have an answer to that. Not his fault.

Interviewer: Do you feel you were treated fairly?

P4: Fairly, yes. Well, is anybody treated fairly? The ones who make noise get on faster, I have seen it. My neighbour's daughter is one of those who writes letters to the chief executive and she gets things done. Good luck to her. It is not in me to do it and I am not going to start now.

Interviewer: If you could change one thing?

P4: Tell people the truth at the start. If it is a year, say a year. Do not say eight weeks because that is what the target says. Everybody knows it is not eight weeks. When you tell a man eight weeks and it is fifty, you have not just made him wait, you have made him a fool for believing you.

Interviewer: Anything else you want to add?

P4: Only that the staff themselves were good. Every one of them. I do not want this to be another moan about the health service, because when I finally got in there they could not have done more for me. It is the getting in that is broken, not what happens once you are through the door.
