Interviewer: You were supporting your mother through this, is that right?

P5: Yes. I am her daughter and I am her interpreter and I am her advocate and I am also a full-time teacher, so I was doing all of it in my lunch breaks. She was referred in the February for the memory problems.

Interviewer: What was the process like?

P5: It was a disgrace, and I do not use that word lightly. Four different departments, none of whom talked to each other. I filled in the same form three times. Three times I gave them her date of birth, her medications, the same history, and every time it was as if we were starting from zero. At one point they sent a letter to an address she left in 2009.

Interviewer: How did you respond?

P5: I escalated. I wrote to PALS, I copied in the practice manager, I asked for a named coordinator. And I got one, eventually, and after that it was better. So the system does work, but only if you know the words. You have to know that PALS exists. You have to know to ask for a named contact. My mother would never have known any of that and she has been paying into this system for fifty years.

Interviewer: What was the impact on her?

P5: She stopped asking about it. That is what I noticed. For the first two months she asked every week, has the hospital rung. Then she stopped asking. And I do not think she forgot, I think she decided it was not coming. Watching someone give up on being helped is a particular kind of awful.

Interviewer: And the impact on you?

P5: I was angry all the time. I was short with my students, I was short with my husband. I resented the hospital and then I felt guilty for resenting them because you can see the staff are drowning. Every person I spoke to sounded exhausted. It is very hard to be furious with somebody who is clearly doing three people's jobs.

Interviewer: Did she eventually get the assessment?

P5: In the October, so eight months. And the assessment itself was excellent. The nurse who did it was extraordinary, she spent two hours with mum, she spoke to her not to me, which almost nobody does. If the whole thing had been like that I would be sitting here telling you a completely different story.

Interviewer: What needs to change?

P5: One record. One person who owns the case. And stop making families do the coordination, because families do it for free and then you never find out how broken the joins are. I am the reason my mother got seen. That should not be a sentence anyone has to say.

Interviewer: Would you say the delay caused harm?

P5: Yes. Not dramatic harm, nobody died. But eight months of a woman not knowing what is wrong with her mind, and eight months of not starting anything that might have helped. You cannot get those months back and nobody is accountable for them. They are just gone.
